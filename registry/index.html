    Rules¶

    1. Form is beat<scene>_shot<NN>, NN zero-padded from 01.
    2. Path-safe beat ids. The beat sheet writes 3-base and 3-halluc with a hyphen the other rows don't have. In a path they normalise to beat3base and beat3halluc. The beat sheet keeps its own display labels; only the identifier changes.
    3. No descriptive ids. beat2b_deglove is a description living in a path component. A human-readable name belongs in shot.json beside the id, never as the id.
    4. Zero-shot beats get no shot directories. Beats 3-base and 4 are pure gameplay with 0s of footage.
    5. Numbers are assigned in story order and do not renumber. If a shot is cut, its number is retired rather than reused; if one is added, it takes the next free number regardless of where it sits in the cut. Renumbering breaks every path that ever referenced the old one. STRUCK/leal-renumber-rule-2026-10-21: Leal, 2026-10-21 11:11, typed in Adam's session: "they must always use proper numbering conventions...so they must renumber shots for newly added shots and note the old numbers". Shot ids are numbered in cut order. A newly added shot takes its place and the later shots renumber; every renumbered shot's previous id is noted. Scope: shot ids only. STRUCK/leal-renumber-rule-2026-10-21: Leal, typed in this session on 2026-10-21: "yes the renumber rules includes clue ids" -- the rule covers shot ids AND clue ids; the NC1a suffix convention is superseded. OPEN for Leal, not decided: what a CUT shot's number does (retire, or close the gap).

    The crate pass — NO NEW SHOT, but shot01 is restaged¶

    Ruled 2026-10-03 (Sam), routed via Soren. 2b stays 6 shots / 33s. The beat sheet does not move.

    Kara's reading that the pass sits inside the existing approach coverage is right, but not at the staging shot01 currently has. The damage has to read in motion — shell burst outward, webbing snapped rather than unclipped, lid torn off its hinges — and that is what rules out "NAIC got here first" with no dialogue. Snapped webbing is a detail. At the scale of a ten-metre approach wide it is simply not resolvable, so the shot would carry the crate and lose the thing the crate is for.

    So: the case becomes a large near-foreground element and she crosses behind it without slowing. The crate is big enough in frame for the break to read; she is smaller, further back, and does not alter her line.

    This is better than the wide for three reasons beyond legibility:

    • shot01 currently has no foreground layer. This gives it one, and depth.
    • "She passes it without slowing" only reads as a choice if the crate is prominent enough that passing it is one. At distance she isn't ignoring it, she's just walking.
    • The crate and her indifference to it become the same image, which is what Kara is actually after.

    One honest cost, flagged rather than buried: a near-foreground prop must survive scrutiny. The case has to be built to a higher standard than a mid-ground dressing piece — the webbing, the hinge tear and the burst direction all get looked at. Same prop count, more prop quality. Worth checking against the shootdown sequence's green chest, since it is the same object twenty minutes later and ruined.

    Not restaged to a crouch or an inspection, per Kara — a woman who checks the crate is doing her job, and the scene is about the other thing.

    Tier is a scene-level column; the Tier 1 requirement is shot-level¶

    Adam's shot.json proposal to carry tier and discharges_tier_requirement as separate fields is correct, and the reason is sharper than bookkeeping.

    The beat sheet assigns tier per beat. GDD §12.4 defines tier per shot — "close-up emotion; facial/emotional performance shots." Those are different grains, and 2b is exactly where they come apart:

    • Scene 2b is Tier 1 because it contains facial performance — shot03 and shot06.
    • beat2b_shot04, the deglove, does not discharge the Tier 1 requirement. Not because of framing — I argued its camera must hold her face, and it should — but because there is no facial performance in it to hold. The torso, head and legs are bit-identical across all 168 frames; Leal ruled previs is hands-only and the generative step carries the performance. A face in frame doing nothing is not a facial-performance shot.

    So beat2b_shot04 is a capability proof — it answers the finger-animation gap, which is Leal's stated reason for running it first, and that gap does block everything. It is not the Tier 1 shot GDD §12.4:382 requires as a deliverable, and the requirement stays open against a named facial-performance shot — beat2b_shot03 on current reading.

    Collapsing those two fields is precisely the failure §12.4:382 pre-registers: a demo that "would look like it validated the thesis while leaving the highest-risk category completely untested."

    One consequence worth carrying forward: beat2b_shot04's own legibility depends on performance the previs layer cannot supply. I argued the face must be in frame because the deglove is where she decides — and the decision will be carried entirely by the generative step, against no previs reference for the face. That makes it a harder shot than its hands-only previs suggests, and it is an argument for beat2b_shot03 being proven early rather than late.
